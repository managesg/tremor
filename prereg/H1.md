## 1. Hypothesis

The chance that an idle capability is used again falls with idle time faster than a memoryless
process predicts, and therefore a decay window tau in {1, 3, 7, 14, 30} days exists at which
`grow_decay` cuts idle exposure by at least 30% against `promote_repeated` while adding at most
10% more prompts, on sessions held out from the tau choice.

## 2. Concept and status

Usage-based decay (Anderson and Schooler / ACT-R base-level activation) applied to least
privilege (Saltzer and Schroeder). Status: interpretation. The decay law is established for
human memory; its transfer to capability reuse is what is tested.

## 3. Prior art checked

Decay on idle: Android auto-reset, AWS unused-access analysis. Grow on need: NVIDIA OpenShell
Policy Advisor, JIT access. The grow/shrink session policy with a two-week unused rule is
described in CJ Avilla's talk (talk claim, owner-validated). Our claim is only the measurement
on coding-agent traces. A combined, risk-tiered rule was "not found in my search".

## 4. Data

- Source: the owner's local Claude Code transcripts, the corpus T1 already reads.
- Licence: private, the owner's own data. Our analysis exports nothing; salted aggregates only.
  (The recorded sessions already sent their content to the model API when they ran.)
- Excluded: all message text. Only capability keys, timestamps, session ids and denial markers.
- No public substitute: public agent trajectories do not record permission prompts as far as

- Unit of analysis: session, meaning a main session id. Subagent events carry their parent's

## 5. Splits

By time, by session: tau is chosen on the first 70% of sessions and judged on the last 30%
(E1.1). No session in both parts. Hash of split file: recorded at freeze.

## 6. Arms and baselines

Replay of the same event stream through the four policies in `ledger.py`: `grow_decay` (the
arm), `promote_repeated` (the comparator; the answer the owner received in the Q&A), static
allowlist, always-ask. The last three ship today. The auto-mode classifier cannot be replayed
offline (E5.3 covers it live). E1.3 adds the allowlist-mining hold-out as the AWS-style baseline.

## 7. Primary metric (one)

Relative reduction in idle exposure on the held-out sessions, in percent:
`100 * (E_promote_repeated - E_grow_decay) / E_promote_repeated`, with `E` in rule-days as
defined in SCIENCE section 4 (idle rule-days summed over every gap while the rule stands; grace
`g` = 0, the SCIENCE default). One definition for all policies (E1.0 must be merged first).

Guardrail, part of the pass rule and not a second primary: relative change in prompts per 100
calls, `grow_decay` against `promote_repeated`, in percent; must be at most +10%.

Capability key: the current coarse key `Bash:<first token>` is primary (design choice: it is
the key the built code uses). PLAN 7.1 requires a finer granularity to be reported as well; its
definition is not in PLAN and must be written here before the freeze.

## 8. Secondary metrics (exploratory)

Standing rule-days; thrash rate; grant-use ratio; after-denial classes; false-deny and
excess-grant from E1.3; the reuse-hazard fit (E1.2): power law against exponential by
likelihood ratio on per-key gaps, censored gaps handled as censored (SCIENCE 4).

## 9. Minimum effect of interest

30% less idle exposure at no more than 10% more prompts. Below that, decay trades exposure for
prompts close to one for one and is not worth a policy change the owner must sign. Both figures
are PLAN's.

## 10. Sample size and stopping rule

- Floor: at least 200 real permission events and at least 30 sessions. PLAN does not say
  whether the floor applies to the whole corpus or to the held-out part. Read here as the whole
  corpus (design choice: it is the literal wording). At exactly 30 sessions the held-out part
  is 9 sessions (30% of 30), which the result must state.
- n is whatever the corpus holds at freeze; it is fixed at freeze and printed. No extension
  after looking (SCIENCE 5).
- Stop rule: below the floor, report "precondition not met: untestable on this corpus" and stop
  (WORKPLAN wave 2, 1.2).
  This fired on the local corpus (1 session; see "Pre-freeze observation").

## 11. Analysis

- Paired difference between policies on the same held-out sessions.
- Cluster bootstrap resampling sessions; 95% percentile interval (PLAN section 2); 10,000
  resamples (SCIENCE 5).
- H1 counts only if the interval of the primary metric excludes 0 and the point estimate is at
  or above 30%, with the guardrail point estimate at or below +10% (SCIENCE 5 rule).
- Correction: H1 is one of five confirmatory hypotheses (H1-H4, H6), Holm-corrected as one
  family (PLAN); family level 0.05 (SCIENCE 5).
- The p-value entered into Holm is a design choice (PLAN fixes the interval method, not a
  test). It is the one-sided bootstrap p-value: the share of the 10,000 resamples in which the
  primary metric is at or below 0.
- `stats.paired_bootstrap` returns only (point, lo, hi) today, so this p-value needs a code
  change before the freeze.
- Hazard: likelihood ratio, power law against exponential, bootstrap over sessions. "Flat"
  means the exponential is not rejected: the interval of the log-likelihood ratio includes 0
  (design choice: PLAN says "flat" without a test; this is the test E1.2 already names).

## 12. Kill criteria

Any one kills H1:
1. The corpus named at the freeze has fewer than 200 real permission events, or fewer than 30
   sessions. Applied before a freeze, as on the local corpus on 2026-11-19, this marks H1
   **untestable on that corpus**, not refuted (owner 2026-11-19: H1 stays pending).
2. Denials not reliably detectable in the transcripts.
3. No tau in {1, 3, 7, 14, 30} days meets the 30% / 10% bar on held-out sessions.
4. The reuse hazard is flat. Then decay only trades exposure for prompts one for one.

## 13. Sanity checks before the main run

- E1.0 regression test passes: a key used on days 0-3 and again on day 40 scores the same idle
  definition under every policy (the PLAN example: 1 idle day against 15 under the old code).
- No session in both split halves; tau picked on the first part only (poisoned-test fixture).
- Event and session counts printed and above the floor.
- Token accounting: not applicable (no model).

## 14. Known threats to validity

- Replay is counterfactual: claims are limited to prompts and exposure, not behaviour.
- Prompt counts are an upper bound: transcripts do not show which calls were really prompted.
- One user's traces: a case study. E1.4 (deferred to after G8) is the only cure.
- The capability key is coarse; a finer key changes every number.
- Tau is chosen from five values on the first part; the held-out judgment guards against that.

## 16. What would change our mind

- For decay: a tau meeting the bar on held-out sessions and a hazard steeper than exponential.
- Against: any kill above. A flat hazard is the strongest, because it removes the mechanism.
- A pass on one person's corpus does not license a general claim; other users' salted reports
  that miss the bar would overturn it.
