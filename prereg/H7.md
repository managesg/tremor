## 1. Hypothesis

On Qwen3.5-0.8B, the released direction edit (arm A) beats each decoding-only fix, thinking
off (arm B) and a repetition penalty (arm C), by at least 5 points of MATH-500 accuracy at the
same 8,192-token per-problem cap. Its off-target loss against the same decoding arm is at most
2 points. (PLAN, except the cap value: owner 2026-11-19. PLAN gives 32,768; see section 17.)

The author's stated expectation is that H7 is killed (PLAN).

## 2. Concept and status

Model editing (ROME, MEMIT, task vectors, activation steering, abliteration) and the MIT
"weight-space programming" post of 2 Nov 2026. Status: interpretation. The edit type is
established; whether it does more than a decoding setting is the open point.

## 3. Prior art checked

Research file 08 (abstracts opened). Abliteration is the closest precedent. The original has
one editing run per budget and no LoRA, steering or decoding baseline. A matched-cap comparison
of the released edit with decoding-only fixes was "not found in my search".

Second-hand leads, not facts (08 section 6, none opened):
- The edited model reaches 51.0% on the full MATH-500 in an independent re-run (also the model
  card's claim, G3i).
- Thinking-off base reaches 52%.
- A non-thinking baseline reaches 43.95%.
- A repetition penalty "recovers 54%".
- A community steering vector reaches 36 of 96 correct, against 10 for a random direction.

The critics' reading is that most of the gain is a loop fix (08: "predominantly loop repair";
the authors' own Gemma result agrees). That reading is why we expect the edit and decoding to
land close together.

Stages (owner 2026-11-19; the order inside stage 1 is a design choice):
- **Stage 1:** arm B, then arm A, then arm 0. The gate needs only B and A. Arm 0 is the
  reference and the most expensive arm, so it runs last: a cap or memory stop during arm 0
  leaves the gate decision intact.
- **Stage 2:** arms C, D and D-null on the same 100 problems, and the off-target checks. It
  runs only if the stage-1 gate passes (section 12). The stage-1 generations are never
  regenerated or re-scored with changed code; their file sha256 values are recorded when stage
  1 ends.

Arm C details:
- The presence penalty 1.5 is already part of the published `think35` (WORKPLAN D3; G9 section 0, item 4).
  Arm C therefore differs from arm 0 only by r.
- r is chosen from {1.05, 1.1, 1.2}. 1.05 is the authors' own repetition-penalty value
  (`profiles.py`, the `lfm25` profile). 1.1 and 1.2 are design choices.
- Selection is on 20 seeded practice problems from `math_train.jsonl`. The winner is the value
  with the highest accuracy under the rule in section 7; ties go to the smaller r.
- The chosen r is written into this file before the freeze.
- If no candidate beats r = 1.0 (arm 0's setting) on the practice problems, the smallest
  candidate is still used, so that C remains a distinct decoding arm. This is recorded as a
  deviation (design choice).

Arm D details (design choices unless marked):
- For each layer l in {15, 19}, a unit direction u_l is computed. It is the mean of the
  `down_proj` output on "loop" token positions minus the mean on "non-loop" positions, from
  arm-0-style generations on practice problems. The loop and non-loop definitions are open
  (WORKPLAN D3; section 18, item 2).
- The edit selects the 64 columns j of `W_l` (shape [1024, 3584]) with the largest
  `|u_l . W_l[:, j]|`, then sets `W_l[:, j] <- W_l[:, j] - u_l (u_l . W_l[:, j])`.
- The result has 64 x 1,024 x 2 = 131,072 touched weights. That is the size of the authors'
  p50c edit (G9 2; 08).
- The edit is written to a copy under `runs/`. The base is hashed before and after.
- D-null uses the same column rule and size with u_l replaced by a seeded Gaussian vector,
  normalised. There is one random direction per layer.

Off-target (owner 2026-11-19 extends PLAN's A-C to A-D): the MMLU-Pro subset for arms A, B,
C, D and D-null, with the official prompt and extraction and 1 sample (G9 4). IFEval runs only

What ships today: decoding configs (arms B and C). The optional LoRA step is out of scope; it

## 7. Primary metric (one)

`acc8k(arm)` is the share of the 100 problems where the generation:
1. finished (end-of-sequence, not cut at the cap),
2. within 8,192 generated tokens, and
3. had its answer graded correct.

This is the truncation read of `train/t7_arms.py` `accuracy_at_cap` at cap 8,192 (owner
2026-11-19). A finished generation needs condition 1 explicitly. Today's
`accuracy_at_cap` checks only `correct and tokens <= cap`, so it would credit a `\boxed{}` in a
generation cut at exactly 8,192. The code must add `finished` before the freeze (section 18).

Patch gain at 8k (SCIENCE 4, with the cap above): `acc8k(A) - acc8k(X)` for X in {B, C},
paired by problem, in percentage points. Each comparison is judged separately. The better
decoding arm is never picked on the test data.

## 8. Secondary metrics (exploratory)

- Off-target delta: `acc(A) - acc(X)`, X in {B, C}, on MMLU-Pro and, if run, IFEval. This
  feeds kill 2.
- `acc8k(D) - acc8k(D-null)`, paired. This decides whether the arm D script is kept.
- `acc8k(A) - acc8k(0)`: the published effect's direction at our cap.
- `acc2k` for every arm and `acc32k` for arms generated to 32,768. Both use the same rule
  read from the same generations (PLAN T7: lower caps by truncation).
- Arm 0 at 32,768 against the published 7.9%, and arm A at 32,768 against the published 48.1%
  (08). These are descriptive replication checks. The published figures average 4 samples on
  all 500 problems (G9 3); ours use 1 sample on 100. Our 95% interval is about ±5 points
  around 8% and about ±10 points around 48% (binomial, n = 100).
- Patch size: weights changed over total weights.
- Cut share: the fraction of generations stopped at the cap, per arm.

## 9. Minimum effect of interest

5 points on MATH-500, and at most 2 points lost off-target (PLAN). Below 5 points, a decoding
config does the same job with no weight change to sign, ship and roll back.

## 10. Sample size, power and stopping rule

- n = 100 MATH-500 problems per arm, one sample each, the same problems in every arm (owner
  2026-11-19). This replaces PLAN T7's "full MATH-500 for A-D" (section 17).
- Arm 0 at 32,768 generates at most 100 x 32,768 = 3.3M tokens (WORKPLAN 6).
- Power, by normal approximation for a paired binary difference. Not simulated. The assumption
  is a discordance of 20-40%, meaning the share of problems where exactly one of the two arms
  is right. Probabilities are per comparison. H7 must win both comparisons, so its joint chance
  of surviving is lower.

| True A - X | P(survive) | P(kill 1 fires) | Most likely outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| -4 points (decoding better; the leads in section 3 point this way) | about 0 | 30-52% | inconclusive or kill |
| 0 | about 2% | 12-20% | inconclusive (about 80%) |
| +5 | 12-20% | about 2% | inconclusive |
| +10 | 36-63% | about 0 | either |
| +15 | 68-95% | about 0 | survive |

- Interval half-width at n = 100: about ±9 to ±12 points. The minimum detectable effect, at 80%
  power for an interval above 0, is about 12-18 points.
- The authors' 7.9% to 48.1% is the edit against arm 0, about 40 points. n = 100 detects an
  effect of that size with near certainty (secondary `A - 0`).
- That effect is not the H7 contrast. Against decoding fixes, the leads put the realistic
  effect near 0 (section 3). At n = 100 the design cannot separate the edit from a decoding fix
  within about ±10 points.
- The likely result is therefore "inconclusive". Section 12 says what that means.
- For reference, at n = 500 (PLAN's original) kill 1 would fire 42-71% of the time at a true
  difference of 0, and the minimum detectable effect would be about 6-8 points.
- The off-target subset sizes are proposed at 200 MMLU-Pro questions and 100 IFEval prompts
  (design choice; fixed in section 18). At those sizes the off-target interval is about ±8
  points, so a 2-point loss cannot be resolved. Kill 2 would also fire by chance on a no-loss
  edit about 50-75% of the time across its comparisons (section 12).
- n is fixed in advance and never shrunk to fit the cap (WORKPLAN wave 2, 8.2).
- Stop rule: a hard cap of 12 GPU-hours for G9 (PLAN 6.2), covering both stages together. Stop
  and report if it is reached. Results are per-problem JSONL, so a stop leaves a valid partial
  result. It is reported as partial, not as a test. If the smoke projection for stage 1 exceeds
  the cap, ask the owner. Time spent in runs the memory watchdog killed counts toward the cap.

Stage-1 gate: derivation and operating characteristics.
- The audit's interval half-width at n = 100 is 1.96 x sqrt(d / n), with d the discordance:
  8.8 points at d = 20% and 12.4 points at d = 40%. That is the "±9 to ±12" above.
- The noise margin is the upper end of that range, 12 points (design choice: the conservative
  end, so the gate does not depend on the discordance we happen to observe). "More than 12
  points" means A finishes and solves at least 13 more of the 100 problems than B.
- Chance that the gate's 13-problem condition is met, by the same normal approximation (SE =
  sqrt((d - delta^2) / n), continuity-corrected, not simulated; the range is d = 20% to 40%):

| True A - B | P(gate passes) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0.3-2.4% |
| +5 | 5-12% |
| +10 | 28-34% |
| +15 | 66-72% |
| +20 | 89-97% |

- The gate passes with at least 89% probability only for a true difference of about 20 points
  or more. The leads in section 3 put the true difference near 0 or below. The expected stage-1
  outcome is therefore a stop, as inconclusive or as a kill.

## 12. Decision rule and kill criteria

**Staged rule (owner 2026-11-19; the numeric gate was derived in section 10 as a design choice
and the owner accepted the 12-point value on 2026-11-19).**
Stage 1 is read once, after arms B and A are complete on all 100 problems:
1. **Kill at stage 1:** kill 1 below fires against B (the upper bound of the 95% interval of
   `A - B` is below +5 points). H7 is killed. Stage 2 does not run.
2. **Gate passes:** `acc8k(A) - acc8k(B)` is more than 12 points (at least 13 problems of 100)
   **and** the 95% interval of `A - B` lies above 0. Stage 2 runs, if its projected GPU-hours
   fit what is left of the 12-hour cap. If they do not fit, ask the owner; n is not shrunk and
   no arm is dropped silently.
3. **Otherwise: inconclusive at stage 1.** Stop. Stage 2 does not run. This is a legitimate
   final result, reported as "not shown" with the interval.

Notes on the staged rule:
- H7 survives only by beating both B and C, so a stage-1 stop cannot hide a survival. What a
  stop gives up is the chance that arm C would have turned "inconclusive" into "kill", and all
  knowledge about arms C and D. The architecture consequence of both outcomes is the same
  (below), so no decision is lost.
- The gate is stricter than the survive condition against B. A result with the interval above
  0 and a point estimate between +5 and +12 stops at stage 1 as inconclusive. This is deliberate
  (design choice): three more arms are not spent on an effect inside the noise margin.
- The A-against-B comparison is not re-tested after stage 2. One look, no extension of n.
- Off-target checks run in stage 2 only. Kill 2 can therefore fire only after the gate passes;
  it exists to qualify a survival.
- Arm 0 does not enter the gate. If arm 0 is cut short by the cap or by memory, the gate result
  stands and the 7.9% check is reported as partial.

The rule below applies against X = B at stage 1 and against X = C at stage 2.

**Survive:** for both B and C, the 95% interval of `A - X` lies above 0, the point estimate is
at least +5 points (SCIENCE 5), and kill 2 does not fire.

**Kill** (either one; PLAN):
1. A decoding-only arm is within 5 points of the edit. Operationally (design choice; PLAN
   says only "within 5 points (paired CI)"): for B or C, the upper bound of the 95% interval of
   `A - X` is below +5 points. This also covers a decoding arm that beats the edit.
2. The edit loses more than 2 points off-target against the same decoding arm.
   Operationally: on any off-target subset that was run, against B or C, the point estimate of
   `A - X` is below -2 points.
   - The point estimate is PLAN's wording. With an interval of about ±8 points it fires by
     chance in roughly 30% of single comparisons at a true difference of 0 (normal
     approximation).
   - It can fire on up to four comparisons (B and C, times MMLU-Pro and IFEval). The joint
     chance of killing an edit with no real off-target loss is therefore about 50-75%. That is
     an estimate under correlation between comparisons, not computed exactly.
   - A kill from kill 2 alone is reported as "killed by guardrail; a 2-point loss is unresolved
     at these sizes".
   - The rule is strict against the edit, which is the direction PLAN expects.
   - If IFEval is not approved, kill 2 rests on MMLU-Pro alone: two comparisons, about 50%.

**Inconclusive** (design choice; PLAN names no third outcome): neither of the above. It is
reported as "not shown", never as "refuted" or "supported". The architecture consequence is
the same as for a kill (design choice: no weight edit enters without survival):
- "The hub edits cell weights" stays out of the architecture.
- Cells get decoding configs and, where trained, adapters.
- The P7 LoRA arm does not run, because it requires survival.

Kill consequence: as above, stated as a refutation.

Separate, not a kill of H7: if the 95% interval of `D - D-null` does not lie above 0, the arm D
script is dropped (PLAN T7). This does not kill arm A.

## 14. Known threats to validity

- One model and one published edit. A kill says the edit adds nothing over decoding on this
  model's loop problem. It says nothing about weight edits in general. A survival does not show
  that a hub can find such edits (arm E is not run).
- The 8,192 cap is arm B's native published cap. Thinking-on arms, A among them, are truncated
  at it: the model card's own re-run has 31.6% of the edit's answers hitting 32,768 (G3i). This
  is the matched-cost question by design, not a flaw, but `acc8k(A)` will sit below 48.1%.
- Batched generation draws all problems from one random stream. Pairing is by problem, not by
  identical random draws.
- One random direction per layer for D-null. A single null draw is noisy.
- Arm C's r is chosen on 20 practice problems, so the choice itself is noisy.
- The off-target subsets are small (section 10). GPU-only power excludes CPU, memory, storage
  and supply loss.
- Pre-freeze exposure of the main set (section 16a).
